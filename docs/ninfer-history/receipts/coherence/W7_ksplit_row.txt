WHY (mechanism, from the kit + census -- not narrative): Team Green's KSC4 won because THEIR wide
arm ran ONE CTA/SM (fat 272-tile chains, 80 CTAs on 36 SMs) -- splitting k DOUBLED the resident-CTA
count, and their consume proved the pattern wall yields to raw concurrency (103.8 -> 137.4 GB/s).
OUR V0 is the opposite posture: 128-VGPR-class resourcing already holds 2 blocks/CU (224-544 CTAs =
2.0-4.9 waves on 3 of 4 geometries), so k-split CANNOT raise co-resident concurrency -- the extra
CTAs queue, and each pays its own LDS-table prologue more often (S x more prologues over shorter
chains) plus the fp32 partials write+reduce (~0.2-0.4 ms). The limiter kit closes the loop: (a)
V0 is AT its pattern wall at TODAY's co-residency, (b) time is linear in per-CTA k-work, (c) M is
not the binding axis -- so the only lever that could pay is more CO-RESIDENT CTAs, and on gfx900 at
128 VGPR / 2-blocks-per-CU the k-split grid trick does not buy residency (unlike their 1-CTA/SM
posture). Consistent with the R2 citations: mad-mix and padded-swizzle proved V0's conversion stream
is the latency-hiding material -- this row proves the SAME stream also fills the co-residency that
k-split would need to expand. The family is closed for the V0 posture on gfx900/ROCm-6.2.
